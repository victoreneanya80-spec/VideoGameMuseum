About the Game

Tetris was created by Soviet software engineer Alexey Pajitnov in 1984 while he was working at a computer research center in Moscow. Shapes made of four squares fall from the top of a well, and the player rotates and slides them so they form complete horizontal lines. Each completed line disappears, and the game speeds up as you go.

The name combines "tetra," the Greek prefix for four, with tennis, Pajitnov's favorite sport.

Why It Belongs in the Museum

  • Its rules fit in one sentence, yet it can be played for a lifetime.
  • Bundled with the Game Boy in 1989, it helped turn handheld gaming into a mass market.
  • It has been released on almost every gaming platform ever made.
  • There are exactly seven pieces, called tetrominoes: I, O, T, S, Z, J and L.
